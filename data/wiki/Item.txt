An item is an object that can exist within inventories of players, mobs or armor stands; in various containers (like chests or droppers), as well as in item frames, glow item frames, and shelves.

Behavior
Items usually exist in the player's inventory, or any external inventories like chests. When hovered or selected in a GUI, items display their name and additional information in a tooltip. Players can move items through the functionality of the GUI — like smelting in a furnace or equipping in armor slots — or put them in the hotbar. During gameplay, the player can select a hotbar slot to hold the item in the hand, while it briefly displays its name on the HUD, in order to use it.

Some items when , place a block or entity (minecart, spawn eggs, etc.) version of themselves into the game world. Put simply, they are an item when in the inventory, and a block when placed. For example, boats turn into an entity when placed, and cacti turn into a block when placed. There are also items which can be used for specific functionality (like consuming food), or items that have no use but are required for recipes (like smelting) to create other items.

Thrown items
Blocks and entities can drop items into the world when broken/killed or in specific circumstances. This is a dropped item entity represented by a sprite that floats above the ground. Players can also choose to  held items or any item in a GUI. It remains for 5 minutes in a loaded chunk before despawning (except for the Nether star), unless the player walks over it to pick it up, it is picked up by a mob, hopper or minecart with hopper, or it is destroyed by fire, lava, cactus, explosion, void, or .

Dropped netherite items cannot be destroyed by fire or lava, and dropped Nether stars cannot be destroyed by explosions.

A submerged object ascends toward the water's surface. If the water is flowing, the object is pushed along with it.

Hoppers draw in any items that are placed above them if they are not powered by redstone.

Stacking
Items, both in a GUI and dropped, can either stack up to 64 or 16, or not stack at all:

Most items can stack to 64. This includes all full blocks, whose outlines take up an entire block when placed.
Some items, such as snowballs, empty buckets, eggs, signs, and ender pearls, can stack up to a maximum of 16.
Some other items do not stack at all, such as anything with a durability (weapons, armor, and tools), filled buckets, and potions.
All items can technically be forced to stack up to 99 or 127 using commands.

Items with use in the world
These items, when highlighted in a player's hotbar (or "held"), in the off hand, or equipped in an armor slot, can be used by either  or , or can serve a specific purpose (for example, offer the player advantage or disadvantage). Some can be used any time, others only when aiming at specific blocks or entities.

Items with indirect use in the world
The player cannot interact with or directly use these items; however, they are used for trading, brewing, enchanting, or crafting ingredients for other items that do have direct uses. Clocks and recovery compasses are exceptions, as they merely serve an informative function (although clocks also attract piglins as a dropped item entity).

Spawn eggs
Spawn eggs spawn the entity inside them. They cannot be obtained in Survival mode.
