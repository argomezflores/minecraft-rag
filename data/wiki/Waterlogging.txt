TITLE: Waterlogging
CATEGORY: Gameplay
URL: https://minecraft.wiki/w/Waterlogging
---
thumb|x150px|right|A waterlogged oak fence.

Waterlogging is the mechanic that allows non-cube blocks (that is, blocks that do not completely fill their block volume, such as ladders, fences, and plants) to be filled with a water source block. Both the non-cube block and the water source block occupy the same space. If the non-cube block is destroyed, the water source block still remains in the original spot it came from.

, generally all non-cube blocks can be waterlogged and none can be used to displace water source blocks, although they can still displace flowing water.

Properties
, certain blocks have a  block state that can be . If a waterlogged block is pushed by a piston or sticky piston, the waterlogged state of the block is typically removed. However, if a sticky piston gets powered by a short pulse so that the sticky piston spits out the block when pulling, the block retains its waterlogged state.

, waterlogging is handled by the game's layers system. Through editing, any block can be waterlogged, as well as other features such as placing multiple slabs into a single block. In normal gameplay, a player cannot displace water source blocks using doors or other non-cube blocks to create breathing spaces underwater, like one can do . Only flowing water can be displaced .

Like water source blocks, all waterlogged blocks are non-flammable and explosion-proof, even if said waterlogged block is flammable. Waterlogged blocks can also interact with lava to produce cobblestone or obsidian, although lava cannot convert waterlogged blocks to stone. Unlike water sources, waterlogged blocks are immune to freezing.

Water can flow from a non-full face, so waterlogged stairs and trapdoors have no water flowing from their solid face. Despite this, interactions between it and lava are still possible.

Behavior
Blocks have different behavior between Java Edition and Bedrock Edition, as shown by the table below.

Red background: This block cannot be placed in water. Attempting to do so replaces the space they are in with water, breaking and dropping them or their corresponding item.
Dark red background: Water cannot be placed in this block at all. When this block is placed in water, it removes water.
Green background: Water can be placed in this block, and this block does not remove the water source.
Dark green background: This block can only exist in its waterlogged state.
Blue background: Water can be placed in this block, this block does not remove the water source, and water can flow through this block.
